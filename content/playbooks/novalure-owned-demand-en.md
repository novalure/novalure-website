<section markdown="1">
<p class="kicker">Recognition</p>
<h2>Do these situations sound familiar?</h2>
<p class="lead">Most agencies do not have a contact problem. They have a context problem — and it shows up every day. Three examples:</p>
<div class="scene"><span class="tag">Situation 1 · The valuation enquiry</span>
<h4>“I just wanted to know what it’s worth.”</h4>
<p>A homeowner requests an online valuation. You receive the enquiry, call, have a pleasant forty-minute conversation — and afterwards you still do not know whether this property will be sold in any foreseeable timeframe. Curiosity and an intention to sell look identical in the inbox.</p></div>
<div class="scene"><span class="tag">Situation 2 · The portal buyer</span>
<h4>Twenty questions — or no reply at all.</h4>
<p>A buyer enquiry via the portal: name, number, “please contact me”. No search area, no budget, no motivation, no timeframe. Either an interview begins on the phone — or the enquiry sits unanswered, because fifteen others arrived that look exactly like it.</p></div>
<div class="scene"><span class="tag">Situation 3 · The old contact</span>
<h4>The property sold — just not with you.</h4>
<p>An owner who said “maybe next year” eight months ago has now sold. Through another agency. The follow-up lived in one person’s head, and that person was on holiday. Nobody did anything wrong — there was simply no dated next step.</p></div>

All three situations share one cause: enquiries arrive without context, are handled without differentiation and are followed up without a system. That is exactly where this playbook starts.
</section>

<section markdown="1" class="pagejob">
<p class="kicker">Economic friction</p>
<h2>What the current way of working really costs</h2>
<p class="lead">Portal fees appear on an invoice. The more expensive items appear nowhere — four of them:</p>
<div class="cost">
<div class="item"><h4>Interchangeability on the portal</h4><p>Whoever only rents demand competes inside a listing view on position and response time. Your own visibility — and the relationship with owners before they decide to sell — is not built there.</p></div>
<div class="item"><h4>Time spent on curiosity, not maturity</h4><p>Valuation enquiries without any classification absorb exactly the hours that mature seller and buyer conversations would need. The diary is full — just not with the right appointments.</p></div>
</div>
<div class="cost">
<div class="item"><h4>One bucket for everything</h4><p>Seller and buyer enquiries land in the same process, although they need different questions, deadlines and next steps. The result: standard replies that serve nobody well.</p></div>
<div class="item"><h4>Lost long-cycle opportunities</h4><p>Owners decide over months, sometimes years. Without an owner within your team and a scheduled resubmission, you lose precisely the instructions that would have cost the least acquisition effort.</p></div>
</div>

How much this amounts to in commission volume differs by market and portfolio — this playbook deliberately does not do that arithmetic for you. What matters is the pattern: it is not the number of enquiries that limits the business, but the number of prepared conversations that come out of them.
</section>

<section markdown="1" class="pagejob">
<p class="kicker">Leak map</p>
<h2>Where context gets lost along the way</h2>
<p class="lead">From first contact to an instruction or buyer conversation, every enquiry passes through five stations. The leaks sit at the hand-offs:</p>
<div class="leakmap">
<div class="leakrow">
<div class="stage"><span class="n">01</span><span class="t">Visibility</span></div><div class="arrow">→</div>
<div class="stage"><span class="n">02</span><span class="t">Enquiry</span></div><div class="arrow">→</div>
<div class="stage"><span class="n">03</span><span class="t">Qualification</span></div><div class="arrow">→</div>
<div class="stage"><span class="n">04</span><span class="t">Follow-up</span></div><div class="arrow">→</div>
<div class="stage"><span class="n">05</span><span class="t">Conversation</span></div>
</div>
<div class="leaks">
<div class="leak"><b>Leak 1→2</b><p>The occasion is lost: valuation, intention to sell, wish to buy or pure information? Without the occasion there is no fitting way in.</p></div>
<div class="leak"><b>Leak 2→3</b><p>Search area, budget, motivation and timing are missing — the four details that turn a contact into an assessable opportunity.</p></div>
<div class="leak"><b>Leak 3→4</b><p>No owner, no date: whether anyone follows up depends on memory and the day’s workload — not on an agreed step.</p></div>
<div class="leak"><b>Leak 4→5</b><p>The conversation starts unprepared: the enquirer repeats what they already wrote, you ask what a form could have clarified.</p></div>
<div class="leak"><b>Leak 5→1</b><p>Later opportunities fall out of the system: “will be in touch” is not a status — it is the end of the trail.</p></div>
</div>
</div>

Here too: every station can work in isolation. A decent portal profile, a hard-working team, a well-kept address book. The business is lost at the hand-offs — and those stay invisible as long as nobody measures them.
</section>

<section markdown="1" class="pagejob">
<p class="kicker">Quality criteria</p>
<h2>What a prepared conversation must show</h2>
<p class="lead">Seller or buyer: a prepared conversation does not start from zero. Six things should be visible beforehand:</p>
<div class="criteria">
<div class="crit"><div class="ic">1</div><p><b>Occasion.</b> Valuation, sale, purchase or information? The occasion sets the tone, the questions and the sensible next step.</p></div>
<div class="crit"><div class="ic">2</div><p><b>Property or search profile.</b> For an owner: which property, which location. For a buyer: search area, property type, rough size.</p></div>
<div class="crit"><div class="ic">3</div><p><b>Motivation.</b> Why now? A change in life circumstances, a need for capital, months of searching — the motive carries the conversation.</p></div>
<div class="crit"><div class="ic">4</div><p><b>Timing.</b> Zero to three months, three to six, later? Maturity decides priority — not how loudly the enquiry arrives.</p></div>
<div class="crit"><div class="ic">5</div><p><b>Budget or decision signal.</b> For buyers, budget fit and financing status as a self-assessment; for owners, the decision situation — does one person decide, or several?</p></div>
<div class="crit"><div class="ic">6</div><p><b>A next step with a date and an owner.</b> Call-back, valuation appointment, search-profile conversation, viewing — agreed and assigned to one person.</p></div>
</div>
<p class="note">These six points are not an interrogation on the phone. Well-built enquiry paths capture them in passing — the enquirer experiences structure, not bureaucracy.</p>
</section>
